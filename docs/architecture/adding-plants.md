# Adding a plant

**Purpose:** source-based checklist for manually implementing a plant in this checkout. This guide describes code paths, not an automatic generator. It is based on the current source; game behavior still needs to be exercised in-game.

## 1. Decide the plant's identity and availability

1. Add a `SEED_*` value in `src/ConstEnums.h` in the plant section, before `NUM_SEED_TYPES`. Do not insert it among existing values: plant types are used as indices into `gPlantDefs` and are serialized in saves. Append new values so existing numbers stay stable.
2. Add the matching `PlantDefinition` at the same position in `src/Lawn/Plant/PlantDefinitions.cpp`. The array is indexed by `SeedType`, so the entry's `.mSeedType` and array position must agree.
3. Choose how the plant becomes available. `LawnApp::HasSeedType` handles progression and store-owned plants. A custom test plant that should ignore progression needs an explicit case there; if availability depends on an image, return false when that resource is missing. Check the trial-stage guard above the switch too.
4. If the plant must appear in the seed chooser, update `NUM_SEEDS_IN_CHOOSER` and `src/Lawn/Widget/SeedChooserOrder.h`. Keep `SeedChooserTypeAtIndex` and `SeedChooserIndexOf` as inverse mappings for the new entry. Preserve the existing stock slots and special cases for Imitater and Sun Magnet. The chooser's position code uses the reverse mapping, so an enum value appended at the end does not automatically land in the right grid cell.

The current Ephraim entry is an example of an appended custom seed: its enum is after the existing plant values, its chooser slot is separate from its enum value, and `HasSeedType` checks that its image loaded. The transparent `properties/ephraim/Ephraim_Great_Lord_RGBA.png` supplies the current art. Run `python3 scripts/create_ephraim_atlases.py` to regenerate the ignored local assets. It produces a separate six-cel idle atlas and four complete horizontal attack atlases: `lance.png` (21 cels), `critical_lance_complete.png` (21), `javelin_complete.png` (7), and `critical_javelin_complete.png` (11). Each attack includes its matching windup, strike, and recovery in a single row; runtime playback selects one atlas for the entire attack. The generator keeps the source crop groups explicit, omits repeated normal ready poses, splits all four follow-through poses separately, and supplies the critical javelin with two ready recovery poses. All cells are 96 by 96 pixels, with the largest atlas 2016 pixels wide. Both plant generators write a local `manifest.json` with the same per-atlas fields: `columns`, `rows`, `cell`, and `source_bounds` as `[x, y, width, height]` for each source crop. This metadata is not read at runtime. Each cel has its own dwell time: preparation and recovery poses hold longer, while slash smears and releases advance quickly. Normal lance, critical lance, javelin, and critical javelin run for 126, 140, 70, and 110 simulation ticks before additional holds. Their anticipation poses are cels 5, 1, 3, and 3, held for 22, 46, 12, and 32 ticks; successful melee impacts stop for 20, 36, 8, and 16 ticks respectively. Miss holds are 5, 9, 2, and 4 ticks. Explicit recoil stops occur on cels 18, 18, 6, and 10 for 26, 42, 12, and 30 ticks. Melee contact occurs on cels 10, 10, 4, and 8; its elapsed tick is derived from the preceding cel dwell times. Lance holds its visible contact pose, leaving the following smear free to advance. Ranged javelins release on cels 4 and 8 without freezing the release smear, then hold the follow-through cels 5 and 9 for 10 and 18 ticks. Ranged normal and critical throws use separate windup holds of 18 and 38 ticks on cel 3, and recoil holds of 18 and 34 ticks on cels 6 and 10. They then return to the first idle cel, held still facing the attack direction for 40 and 80 ticks before another attack. This resting beat does not pause projectiles or independent echoes, and melee timing remains separate. After the entire primary attack and its stops finish, the between-attack intervals are 55, 95, 28, and 65 ticks (0.55, 0.95, 0.28, and 0.65 seconds at 100 Hz). This countdown never runs during an active attack and never waits for echoes. During that interval melee attacks hold their final recovery cel, while ranged attacks use the still resting pose described above; both preserve attack direction without idle breathing; the recovery flag persists in the existing pause flags. Cursor and planting previews use the same 96-pixel cell scale, proportions, horizontal center, and 80-pixel tile baseline as the planted sprite, rather than the old stretched 140-by-90 sizing. Ephraim costs 300 sun, starts with 3500 HP (also his maximum health), and has 80 base damage. Each primary attack and every afterimage attack, including all nested generations, instantly restores 1.5% of his maximum HP at its melee strike or ranged release frame. Healing occurs once per attack, including misses, rather than once per target or projectile collision. At 3500 maximum HP this is 52.5 HP; the existing serialized fractional health remainder carries partial points between attacks. Health is capped at maximum, excess healing is discarded, and dead plants cannot be revived. Melee style damage is 80 for javelin, 140 for critical javelin, 200 for lance, and 280 for critical lance. Each primary or afterimage melee strike snapshots its eligible contact targets before applying damage, then multiplies each target’s style damage by `Board::GetQuadraticZombieDamageMultiplier(target, contactTargetCount)`. This uses the shared target-count quadratic scaling, including the 900,000-sun resistance threshold, ceil(sqrt(targets)) reduction, tier-dependent multiplier ceiling, and Bungee exemption. Every nested melee echo uses the same path and its own contact target count. Animation multipliers are not separately squared; ranged projectiles remain at 125% of base damage (100). Within 200 pixels (2.5 tiles) of the attacking character’s center in either direction, each primary attack and afterimage uses its own animation variant to select the melee damage scale. Each new echo, including nested children, selects the nearest eligible same-lane target from its own fixed visible origin before windup. It chooses a melee style within that reach or one of the two javelin throws beyond it, so a batch can mix melee and ranged attacks. Its chosen complete row and direction remain fixed through recovery; melee contact checks use that same origin and never hit outside its reach. This reach lets him use melee from behind an adjacent Wall-nut, Tall-nut, or another plant. The placement preview uses the same center-based bounds. Ephraim searches the current logical playfield width (`mApp->mWidth`), including the extended visible area in Endless Pool, for the nearest eligible enemy in the same lane; beyond that reach he chooses normal or critical javelin, releasing a projectile on cel 4 or 8 respectively. Both ranged variants always deal 125% of base damage (100), only on projectile collision, with no immediate melee damage. Normal javelins travel at 5 pixels per tick; critical javelins with the longer windup travel at 8 pixels per tick and push surviving targets 24 pixels in the projectile direction (excluding the stationary boss and bungee). The movement loop advances two pixels per tick, easing to one pixel per tick for the last eight pixels; a single push takes 16 ticks (0.16 seconds). Once the push finishes, targets stagger in place for 40 ticks (0.4 seconds). Overlapping charged hits add signed pending displacement, bounded to one board width, and refresh the stop timer. Stagger pauses movement, actions, and animation without ice or butter visuals; another charged hit refreshes the timer rather than adding duration. Movement and animation resume when it expires, and death is not blocked. Zombie save fields 108 and 109 persist the stagger timer and signed pending knockback distance, defaulting to zero in older saves. Ranged echoes select their own throwing variant based on their distance to the target. Projectile save field 110 persists the charged-throw flag; older saves default to an uncharged projectile. The generator extracts the separate horizontal flight sprite from the original sheet into `javelin_projectile.png`. Ranged echoes also throw one projectile each from their visible trail positions; projectiles travel toward the stored attack direction and disappear after one impact or leaving the screen. Each variant has explicit windup, release or impact, recoil, and recovery timing; afterimages never extend the primary interval. Each melee afterimage randomly selects one of the three other complete attack styles, with its own animation clock, windup hold, hitstop, and contact frame. Each Ephraim starts with a 35% afterimage chance; every attack whose afterimage roll fails adds 15 percentage points for the next attack, capped at 100%. Failed rolls also accumulate a tally, capped at five. A successful trigger creates that many afterimages (at least one when the tally is zero), then resets both the chance to 35% and the tally to zero. All echoes from a trigger appear simultaneously at separate trail positions, spaced 28 pixels apart. Five stacks produce five distinct clones, each playing one complete attack; an individual clone never repeats its attack. Each has its own animation clock, windup/hitstop counters, stored attack direction, and one damage event. Melee echoes that hit at least one target roll once at contact using the same per-plant afterimage chance and failure tally as primary attacks. Failed eligible rolls add 15 percentage points and one tally; successful rolls spawn one to five children and reset the shared chance to 35% and tally to zero. The first echo batch has nesting depth zero, and child batches may reach depth three: primary -> first echo -> child -> grandchild -> final child. Depth-three echoes, misses, and ranged echoes do not roll for children. Children appear 28 pixels further behind their parent per sibling, then select their own target, facing, and attack mode from that position. Styles differ from the parent and preceding sibling when eligible choices permit; if the two ranged styles are both excluded, sibling variety takes precedence. They each attack once and retain their own weighted pose timing and damage scale. Children are appended after updating existing echoes to preserve vector references and begin updating on the next tick. Save field 115 stores matching per-echo depths while field 114 keeps its original seven-field records; older saves default echoes to depth zero. New triggers append echoes while older ones continue, with no combined active-echo limit; the five-echo limit applies per trigger. Echo pauses never freeze Ephraim or another echo, and his attacks do not wait for them to finish. Each melee batch chooses styles different from the primary attack and the preceding echo. Each echo chooses its mode independently; ranged siblings alternate throwing styles when both are ranged. Portable save field 116 stores each fixed echo origin without changing the existing echo record; older saves derive origins from their stored direction and trail offset and finish their existing rows. The ranged mode persists in the existing primary and echo pause flag fields; projectile type 17 is appended without changing older projectile IDs. Per-plant chance, failure tally, and pending spawn count persist in portable save fields 111, 112, and 113; field 114 stores the independent active echoes. Saves without the tally recover it from the stored chance; saves without the chance start at 35%. Attempts without a target do not roll or change the chance. These are source and asset-generation details; in-game visual behavior requires runtime review. For a normal progression plant, follow the existing progression order instead of copying that override.

### Female Sniper bow attacks

`SEED_SNIPER_FEMALE` is appended after Ephraim. Existing challenge and zombie seed IDs remain unchanged through an explicit value for the first non-plant seed. Chooser slot 51 exposes the plant when its idle, complete attack and arrow images exist, including Plant Practice. It currently costs 100 sun and uses the default 500 HP.

Run `python3 scripts/create_sniper_female_atlases.py` with ffmpeg available to extract `properties/sniper-female/Sniper_female_RGBA.png` (300x638). The generator creates a standing `idle.png` and twelve separate atlases: three normal bow rows, three critical rows, dodge, and five map rows. Each source-row atlas retains its own frames. Explicit crop bounds exclude labels, credits, alternate palettes and the loose arrow. Only transparent margins are trimmed; every frame is centered and bottom-aligned inside a transparent 64x64 cell. A local `manifest.json` records exact source bounds and frame counts. Generated PNGs and the source sheet remain ignored local assets.

The generator additionally assembles complete normal (17 cels) and critical (21 cels) bow atlases from explicit matching row groups, and extracts the loose arrow as a horizontally stretched 126x12 projectile, approximately matching the firing pose’s arrow length at the plant’s display scale. Original row atlases remain separate; runtime selects one complete atlas and never changes rows during a shot. The resource loader registers all atlases and the arrow. Board, preview and card art mirror the left-facing source to face enemies on the right. The planted sprite and placement preview use a shared scale of `EPHRAIM_DRAW_SCALE * 55 / 48` (about 2.5065x), matching the 120-pixel visible standing height of Ephraim while keeping the tile foot anchor fixed. Arrow launch height follows this scale; card portraits remain fitted to the packet artwork area.

Female Sniper uses forward same-lane targeting through the current viewport, matching Ephraim’s ranged reach (including Endless Pool’s wider viewport) and chooses normal or critical complete bow sequences. Per-cel timing emphasizes the drawn-bow windup for 18/22 ticks, holds the horizontal-arrow firing pose itself for 14/47 ticks, and holds the final recoil pose for 10/14 ticks. The firing pose is normal cel 13 or critical cel 17; it previously lasted only two ticks while the following recovery pose received the long hold. The following ready pose now lasts two ticks, and all three critical arrows launch while the firing pose remains visible. Normal attacks run for 109 ticks before a 30-tick rest; critical attacks run for 172 ticks before a 42-tick rest, shortening complete cycles to 1.39/2.14 seconds at 100 Hz before impact hitstops. A landed arrow pauses its living source plant’s currently active post-release animation for 5/8 ticks, using maximum rather than added holds for overlapping impacts; it does not freeze projectiles or interrupt a new windup or idle. Each arrow travels right at 7 pixels per tick; normal arrows start at 60 direct damage, and each critical arrow starts at 300% of that base damage (180), before armor/body bonuses. Normal attacks release one arrow at cel 13; critical attacks release three distinct arrows beginning at cel 17 and spaced 23 animation ticks apart (0.23 seconds, or 161 pixels center to center at their flight speed, leaving 35 pixels between their 126-pixel sprites before hitstop delays), totaling 540 base damage (900% of normal base damage) per target if all three hit, before armor/body bonuses. Hitstops may delay remaining burst arrows while preserving their count. Normal arrows disappear on impact; each critical arrow pierces every eligible target in its lane, with a dynamic hit-ID list preventing repeated hits and no 32-target pea limit. Both disappear outside the current viewport. Projectile ID 18 remains unchanged. Collision checks retain the leading 12-pixel tip, using the stretched width to locate it; portable saves restore current arrow dimensions on load.

Every landed sniper arrow applies a non-stacking, refreshed three-second wound: healing received is multiplied by 0.25, and a continuous DOT recalculates 1.5% of current body HP per second with a 15 HP/second minimum. Fractional damage carries between ticks. Both direct arrows and DOT damage flying protection, shields, tier bucket armor and helmets before reaching body HP; piercing passes through zombies without bypassing their armor. DOT retains normal damage eligibility and invulnerability checks. Both zombie regeneration and aquarium brain healing use the same healing helper. Direct arrows deal 75% more damage to flying protection, shields, tier bucket armor and helmets, then 85% more damage to the body; leftover damage is converted from armor units to body units when armor breaks. Thus normal arrows deal 105 armor damage or 111 body damage, and each critical arrow deals 315 armor damage or 333 body damage before other existing zombie modifiers. These bonuses do not multiply DOT. Zombie portable field 110 stores wound duration/fractional damage, and projectile field 113 stores the unlimited piercing hit history; older saves default to no wound or hit history.

Idle uses three ready poses from the sheet in a 0-1-2-1 cycle, with each pose held for 32 ticks. Card and placement previews retain the first ready pose. Existing saved `mAnimPing` stores the attack variant and the shooting counter stores progress; plant field 117 stores the hitstop counter, and projectile field 111 stores the source plant ID and field 112 stores the critical-arrow flag, keeping per-arrow damage fixed after launch. Older portable saves default to no hitstop/source attribution and unflagged arrows use normal damage; rendering-only saves upgrade the zero launch rate and non-shooter subclass on load. Source and generated atlas inspection do not establish in-game visual correctness; builds, tests and runtime checks were not run.

## 2. Fill out the plant definition

`PlantDefinition` in `Plant.h` supplies the seed type, optional direct image array, reanimation type, packet image index, sun cost, cooldown, subclass, launch rate, and name key. Start from the closest existing definition in `PlantDefinitions.cpp` and choose each field deliberately:

- Use a reanimation type for an existing plant animation, with `.mPlantImage = nullptr`.
- Use a direct image array for a custom cel-strip or image, with the resource pointer initialized before the plant can be selected.
- Set `.mPacketIndex` to the matching seed packet art when reusing existing art. For custom packet art, inspect `SeedPacket` rendering and add the smallest corresponding resource/rendering change; `mPlantImage` controls the planted plant image, not automatically the chooser card.
- `.mPlantName` is also used to form translated name and tooltip keys (`[NAME]` and `[NAME_TOOLTIP]`). Add matching strings to the appropriate string resource if the built-in fallback is not suitable.
- Set the subclass and launch rate consistently with similar plants; existing systems use them for behavior such as shooter handling and attack timing.

`Plant::GetCost`, `GetRefreshTime`, `GetNameString`, and `GetToolTip` read the definition. Add a specific branch only when the new plant intentionally differs from those shared rules.

## 3. Load custom art, if needed

For an external cel-strip, declare an `IMAGE_*` resource in `src/Resources.h`, define it in `src/Resources.cpp`, and load it in the startup resource extraction path. Set `mNumCols` and `mNumRows` to the actual cell layout before assigning the image into the one-element `Image*` array referenced by the plant definition. Mark custom images with the existing image-sanding helper when needed. Keep user-provided/license-restricted game art out of commits; this repository expects local `properties/` assets.

`Plant::GetImage` reads the definition's image array. For a direct-image plant, set frame timing in `Plant::PlantInitialize` or its animation update path, and check `PlantAnimation.cpp` and `PlantRendering.cpp` for assumptions about state, scale, shadows, and cel selection. Ephraim derives its idle and complete attack atlases from the PNG; keep source crop groups explicit in the generator. `Plant::Animate` advances within the selected complete attack row, and `Plant::UpdateShooting` applies damage when the displayed pose reaches the matching contact frame. If a missing art file should hide the plant, make `HasSeedType` depend on the loaded resource so the chooser cannot select an unusable seed.

## 4. Add gameplay behavior in the owning module

`Plant.cpp` coordinates lifecycle and update flow; most behavior lives in focused `Plant*.cpp` modules under `src/Lawn/Plant/`. Search for the closest plant family and follow its existing state/update path:

- Shooter and projectile behavior: `PlantShooting.cpp`, `PlantTargeting.cpp`, and the relevant `Projectile*` modules.
- Production and collection: `PlantProduction.cpp` and `BoardEconomy.cpp`.
- Special abilities and area attacks: `PlantAbilities.cpp`, `PlantAreaAbilities.cpp`, and `BoardCombat.cpp`.
- State and animation: `PlantState.cpp` and `PlantAnimation.cpp`.
- Placement restrictions and upgrades: `BoardPlacement.cpp`, `PlantingRules.*`, and existing `Plant::IsUpgrade` / upgrade helpers.

Use existing Board, projectile, damage, healing, and targeting helpers when the behavior matches them. Check consumers in challenges and modes when a new plant changes placement, targeting, or eligibility rules. Avoid adding a large switch to the lifecycle coordinator when a focused plant module owns the behavior.

## 5. Check connected surfaces

Before calling the plant complete, inspect the features it touches:

- **Chooser and seed packet:** `SeedChooserOrder.h`, `SeedChooserScreen.cpp`, `SeedChooserRendering.cpp`, `SeedPacket.*`, and `HasSeedType`.
- **Almanac and text:** `AlmanacDialog.cpp`, `Plant::GetNameString`, `Plant::GetToolTip`, and string resources.
- **Store or unlock flow:** `HasSeedType`, purchase enums/catalog, and purchase handling if the plant is bought rather than progressed.
- **Planting behavior:** `BoardPlacement.cpp` plus the mode/challenge restrictions used by that plant.
- **Persistence:** plant seed types and plant state are serialized in `src/Lawn/System/SaveGamePortable.cpp`. If the plant adds persistent fields, preserve defaults for older saves and use the existing tagged-field conventions. Keep enum numbers stable.

## 6. Manual verification checklist

After coding, check the actual target build and, with local game resources, exercise:

1. The seed appears at the intended picker position, has the intended packet art/name/cost, and is selectable only when unlocked.
2. Plant it on valid and invalid cells, including water, roof, night, and upgrade cases that apply.
3. Observe its idle/attack/ability animations, cooldown, target choice, projectiles or effects, damage, and death/removal.
4. Check relevant challenge or game modes and Almanac behavior.
5. Save and resume with the plant present if it adds or changes persisted state.

The opt-in asset-free rules tests can cover pure rule changes, but they do not verify art loading, rendering, picker interaction, or in-game behavior. Follow the repository's normal build/test instructions in [refactoring.md](refactoring.md) when a code check is needed.
